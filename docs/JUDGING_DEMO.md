# Shadow: the judging demo

Start in `queue.html?track=stage` for the company review workspace. `demo.html` remains the full presentation walkthrough. The new flow uses freshly model-induced policies learned from January and February ERP history. Eight preparation calls cost $2.262135 as reported by the CLI backend. The live flow needs no model calls.

## Start

```sh
uv sync
# Only if data/A/client.db and data/B/client.db are missing:
uv run python -m sim.build
uv run python demo_stage.py --port 8795 --data-dir work/judging-final
```

Open `http://127.0.0.1:8795/queue.html?track=stage`.

The saved induced policies are in `data/A/playbook/stage_learned/v1.json` and the corresponding B path. The launcher copies only agent-visible rows before April into its own sandbox, replays historical support with current code, and adds identical constructed May cases at both clients. It never reads hidden keys or truth databases. It never replaces the working client databases or main playbooks.

Stop the server before starting a fresh rehearsal with `--reset`. That flag refuses directories without a labelled stage manifest. Existing sandbox state otherwise survives restarts.

## The 3-minute script

**0:00 to 0:20, History.** “What do you do about a $12.40 short payment? It depends who you work for. Shadow learns each finance team's conventions from reconciliation links, adjustment journals, approvals and emails.” Show 424 historical bank lines for Lucky Quarter, 244 for Meridian, and the saved model induction. Open one evidence example, not the entire rule list.

**0:20 to 0:45, Decision.** “The same receipt goes to both businesses. Lucky Quarter's history suggests writing off small differences. Meridian's proposal sends this kind of shortfall to its AR lead. But Lucky Quarter's clerks used conflicting adjustment accounts. Shadow exposes that uncertainty rather than silently choosing one.” Both can initially be in review. Do not say the unsigned proposal is already approved.

**0:45 to 1:30, Teach.** In the card labelled “3 cases can clear with one answer”, enter **15**. Tick the checkbox that explicitly adopts the rule and account from May 1. Click Preview. “You are approving a policy, not clicking through three receipts.” Show the proposed-to-approved change, effective date and $12.40 / $13.10 / $14.20 adjustments. Click Approve and rerun. Review items fall **9 to 6**. Return to Decision: Lucky Quarter matches and books $12.40 to 6990; Meridian still routes its receipt to the AR lead. The two policies remain separate.

**1:30 to 1:55, Optional second answer.** In the deposit card, enter **50**, preview and approve. Four deposits clear. Two answers have cleared **7 of 9 constructed bank cases**, leaving the two bank-change holds. The starting input is a convenient rehearsal value, not a model-inferred threshold.

**1:55 to 2:20, Control.** “Even an exact amount and reference match does not override a bank-change control.” Show the email evidence and both held payments. The product does not initiate payments.

**2:20 to 2:45, Undo.** If you skipped the deposit step, use Undo latest answer here. All three receipts reopen. If you did both answers, use Full playbook and retract the shortfall sign-off specifically; the four independently approved deposits stay clear. The offline replay demonstrates this exact sequence.

**2:45 to 3:00, Close.** “Our unit of learning is a company policy with evidence. One answer changes future decisions, and every change can be reversed with its affected cases rechecked.” State that history is synthetic and these cases demonstrate behavior, not benchmark accuracy.

## Claims we can defend

- Both initial policies were generated by the existing model induction and repair pipeline from January-February ERP records. The launcher does not author a policy or insert client-specific logic into the executor.
- The new screen exposes source hashes, reconstructed historical examples and disagreement counts. Historical agreement is distinct from current human authorization.
- The receipt sign-off is explicitly a policy adopted going forward. It does not rewrite earlier human decisions or claim the historical account disagreement disappeared.
- The live actions run the full period through deterministic controls and rules, and validate the preview against policy/data changes before applying.
- The seven cleared cases are constructed demo cases, not seven randomly sampled production cases. Do not extrapolate a time-saving percentage, accuracy, or dollars saved.
- B's shortfall rule has limited support and remains proposed. Its review routing is policy-guided; it is not evidence of an approved automatic resolution.
- Role assertions are suitable only for this local prototype. They are not authenticated identities.

## Queue-first rehearsal

2. Select Lucky Quarter Holdings. Verification holds appear first. Open the bank-change email to show why an exact match still needs a check.
3. Select Policy questions, then the shortfall group. Customer names, receipts and $12.40 / $13.10 / $14.20 differences appear together.
4. Set the limit to 15, approve the displayed rule/account, and Preview what changes. The concise preview shows the new policy and affected cases; the full audit diff is expandable. Approve from the queue.
5. Switch to Resolved to inspect the cleared cases. Undo latest policy answer reopens them. Search, company switching, reviewer selection and Refresh queue remain available.
6. Use Walkthrough, Experiment, Playbook and Results for the rest of the presentation. The original run-based correction queue is preserved on non-stage tracks.
